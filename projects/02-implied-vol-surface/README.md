# 02 — Implied volatility surface from NSE option chains

**Proves:** Black-Scholes inversion, numerical root-finding, the volatility smile and term structure, real-world data cleaning
**Level:** 🟡 · **Time:** 1–2 weekends
**Prerequisites:** [QR roadmap](../../roadmaps/quant-researcher.md) Phase 4; [Project 01](../01-monte-carlo-pricer/)

## Goal

Take real NIFTY option prices, back out the volatility the market is implying for each strike and expiry, and plot the resulting surface. You'll see first-hand that Black-Scholes's constant-volatility assumption is wrong — and learn exactly *how* the market prices that wrongness.

## Data

**NSE F&O bhavcopy** (daily EOD, free) for NIFTY index options. See [data sources](../../data/data-sources.md#derivatives-india).

You need for each contract: expiry, strike, option type (CE/PE), settlement or close price, open interest, volume — plus the NIFTY spot (or the near-month future) on the same day.

**Rate:** use the 91-day T-bill yield as a proxy for r (RBI publishes it).

**Pick one trading day** to start. Later, repeat across days to see how the surface moves.

## Steps

1. **Load and filter.** Keep NIFTY index options for one date. Drop contracts with zero volume or very low open interest — their prices are stale.
2. **Use out-of-the-money options only.** OTM puts below the forward, OTM calls above. They're more liquid, and deep ITM prices are dominated by intrinsic value, making IV noisy.
3. **Compute the forward.** Either use the futures price for each expiry, or infer it from put-call parity at the ATM strike: F = K + e^(rτ)(C − P). This sidesteps dividend assumptions.
4. **Implement Black-Scholes pricing** (reuse Project 01) using the forward (Black-76 form):
   C = e^(−rτ)[F·N(d₁) − K·N(d₂)], d₁ = [ln(F/K) + ½σ²τ]/(σ√τ)
5. **Invert for σ.** Solve BS(σ) = market price:
   - Start with **Newton-Raphson** using vega as the derivative.
   - Fall back to **bisection** (or `scipy.optimize.brentq`) when vega is tiny — deep OTM or near expiry — where Newton diverges.
   - Reject prices below intrinsic value; no σ can match them.
6. **Plot the smile** for each expiry: IV against moneyness (K/F or log-moneyness).
7. **Plot the surface:** IV over moneyness × time to expiry, as a 3D surface or heatmap.
8. **Fit a simple parameterisation** per expiry, e.g. a quadratic in log-moneyness, and report the fit error.

## Done when

- [ ] Your solver recovers σ to 1e-6 when fed prices generated by your own BS function (round-trip test)
- [ ] Solver handles failure cases explicitly: price below intrinsic, near-zero vega, non-convergence — with counts reported
- [ ] Smile plotted for at least 3 expiries; surface plotted across all available
- [ ] Short write-up answering: Why is the NIFTY smile skewed (higher IV for low strikes)? How does the skew change with expiry?
- [ ] Data filters (min volume / OI) stated explicitly, with the number of contracts kept vs. dropped

## Common pitfalls

- **Using spot instead of the forward.** Ignoring the carry between spot and futures distorts every IV, more so for longer expiries.
- **Time to expiry in calendar vs. trading days.** Pick one consistently; for weekly expiries the choice matters a lot. NSE options expire at 15:30 IST on expiry day.
- **Stale prices.** EOD settlement prices for illiquid strikes may not reflect a real trade. This is why the volume filter matters.
- **Mixing calls and puts at the same strike** without checking parity — differences reveal data problems or a wrong forward.
- **Newton-Raphson divergence** when starting far from the solution. A good initial guess (e.g. Brenner-Subrahmanyam: σ ≈ √(2π/τ) · C/F for ATM) helps.

## Stretch goals

- Fit **SVI** (stochastic volatility inspired) parameterisation per expiry and check for arbitrage (butterfly and calendar spreads).
- Animate the surface across a volatile month (e.g. around an election result or budget day).
- Compare implied vol with **realised** volatility of NIFTY over the following period — is implied systematically higher? (This is the volatility risk premium.)
- Compute and plot the risk-neutral density from the smile using the Breeden-Litzenberger result.
- Build an **option chain analyser and payoff visualiser**. For one expiry, let the user choose a multi-leg position (straddle, strangle, vertical spread, iron condor) and plot its payoff at expiry, together with the position's Delta, Gamma, Vega and Theta from the implied volatilities in this project. Use the [Greeks cheat sheet](../../cheatsheets/greeks.md) for formulas and check the aggregated Greeks against finite differences of the position value.
